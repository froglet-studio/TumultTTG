# Tumult v2.0 — the run: route, progression and economy

## The hero

A hero is **Human + two bloodlines**, **6 trait cards** to start, **6 Upgrade Points** spent, **26 vitality**, a **15-die pool**. Over a run a hero grows to at most **9 cards, never more than 3 of 9 from one bloodline** (the v1.0 cap, kept verbatim — it is the draft's balance lever).

**Every trait is kept; nine fly (2.105).** Garrett, 2026-11-21: "once a player has 9 cards (equipment, abilities, pets) then any new card does not mean they loose the old ones. it means they leave them behind on the mission. so all old cards are kept, but only 9 cards can be taken into a mission. between missions all old cards can be selected and swapped as a built that suits the new mission. their dice match the cards chosen. upgrades are no longer refunded because the card is not lost." A pilot's **kit** (`h.cards`) is the nine at most that fly the next mission; its **reserve** (`h.reserve`) holds every other trait it has earned, each with the points on it (`T.collection`, `T.holds`). Once the kit holds nine, a reward's trait offer is still drawn as an exchange (from the `swap` stream, so the rest of a run's loot is the same whether a kit is full): the pilot can **keep it in reserve** (`T.takeOfferedCard` with no trait named) or **fly it now** in place of one that stays behind (`T.swapCard`). Nothing is refunded, because nothing is lost: points stay on the trait they were spent on, and can still be spent on a trait in reserve. **Between missions** (on the route, at a reward, a haven or an event, never in a mission) the Kit view brings a reserve trait in for one of the nine (`T.changeLoadout`, `T.loadCard`, the `loadCard` command), so a pilot builds the kit that suits the next stop. The kit keeps the 3-of-9 cap; the reserve does not need to, but every trait in it is one some kit of nine could fly (`T.canCollect`). The dice follow the kit: each awakened card that flies swaps a plain die for its element's (`T.diceSet`), so a kit of sleeping cards rolls plain. Molt and Trade set the old trait aside the same way, a new one of its bloodline in its place (`T.tradeCard`). A pilot's level counts the points on every trait it holds and a card's worth (`CARD_UP`) for each past six, up to nine. `T.removeCard` still refunds every point (2.76.1) for a trait truly lost, such as a companion's; the base game no longer takes one. Skipping a trait still pays 2 ◇; the simulated players pass on full-kit offers, as before. From 2.75 to 2.104 a full kit's exchange gave the old trait up and refunded its points.

**Shared traits (2.42).** A trait can be carried by several clades: written once for a branch of the tree (every primate, every cat) or as a variant in an unrelated animal (Garrett, 2026-11-16). A card lists them in `clades` (`T.shareTrait` adds one). A shared trait counts toward whichever of the hero's clades has room under the cap (`T.cladeSlots`). A hero whose two clades both carry a trait holds it once, and **a trait a pilot holds is never offered to it again** (2.77; Garrett, 2026-11-20: "lets no longer have them gain extra points for drawing the card again. they just have a smaller pool. which gives them more reliability in their late game adaptive swaps"). The draft, a reward and an event draw only from traits the pilot could add, so a two-clade pilot sees a smaller pool and is likelier to meet the traits it wants. From 2.42 to 2.76 a second pick was **1 Upgrade Point bound to the card** (2 until 2.48) (`DOUBLE_UP`, kept in `hero.bound` as `[points, spent]`); a save or a made pilot from then keeps those points, which are added to whatever is already on the card, spent on its P or E before any free point, never on another card, and given back to the card, not the free pool, when a respec takes it off. When the card itself leaves the kit (an exchange, a molt, a trade), its bound points come back as free points with the rest (2.76.1: "when players lose a card they should get their points refunded").

**Two ways to build.** _Quick Build_ takes one of six pre-made heroes (Rosh the Brawler-in-the-Cloud, Ekora the Tidewarden, Nedira the Bulwark, Echo the Nightwing, Karenth the Network, Slingshot the Catapult), each a painted Cosmic Shore pilot: that pilot's name, pair of clades and art. _Draft_ opens packs of 4 cards drawn across your three pools, pick 1, six times; you may pass a pack. Then spend 6 UP on P/E tracks (caps 3/3 per card). Unspent UP carries over.

**Pilots players made (2.72).** Every pilot confirmed from a draft is kept as a recipe of the same shape as a pre-made hero (`T.madeFrom`: name, title, clades, cards, the points on each and any bound point), on the device under `tumult_v2_heroes`, newest first, at most `T.MADE_MAX` (60); one with the same name, clades and cards replaces the older. A new expedition lists them beneath the pre-made heroes, and choosing one builds it with `T.heroFromPrebuilt`, as it was. Every recipe is checked by `T.validMade` before it is shown or played. On the test page and Tumult Online (`src/ui/heroes-net.js`, playtest build only) each player's list is also written to the page's store, so every player of the page can choose it:

| Path                 | What                                                   | Who reads                    | Who writes                      |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| `heroes/<viewer id>` | `{ list: [recipe, …], at }`: that player's made pilots | anyone who can open the page | that player; the owner may tidy |

Store rules for it (added to the page's `db` rules beside the canon ones in `docs/reference/CANON.md`): `{ "path": "heroes", "read": "view", "write": "owner" }, { "path": "heroes/{self}", "read": "view", "write": "interact" }`. Flying someone else's pilot does not copy it into your own list.

**Pilots keep their growth (2.76).** When an expedition ends, won or lost, every pilot a player made and flew comes home with its traits, the points on them and the points it had not spent (`T.progressRecord`), under the same recipe (`madeId`), one more expedition flown (`runs`). Gear and crystals stay with the expedition. Chosen again, it sets out as it came home: up to nine traits, at its level (`T.pilotLevel`), and the missions of that expedition grow to meet it (`docs/reference/SCALING.md`). Online, each player's screen keeps its own player's pilot. A pilot made by someone else, or a pre-made hero, is not kept. A recipe that has flown may hold more than a new pilot's six points (`T.validMade` checks it against the caps instead).

**Squads of two to five (2.76).** One player flies two pilots; two to five players fly one each, at one screen or online (`T.MAX_PLAYERS` 5, `T.pilotsFor`). With more than two pilots, a face, gear ability or duet move that reaches "an ally" lets its player choose which (2.99): the play lists the allies it can reach (standing, and beside you where the face says so) and the player picks one, so the play stays theirs to plan in full. A face whose only target is the ally asks for it; one with a foe or a node of its own lets the player switch the ally before picking (`T.targetsFor`'s `allies`, the pick carried as `ctx.ally`). "All heroes" means every pilot in the squad. Rally and revives reach the nearest ally down (`T.downedAllyOf`).

**Joining an expedition under way (2.79).** On the route, between missions, one more player may join (Garrett, 2026-11-20: "is there a way the two players mid game could save their game, we update, and then I join in?"), up to five (`T.canAddPlayer`, `T.addPlayer`). Online, the newcomer joins with the game's code while the squad is on the route. A lone player's second pilot passes to the newcomer; otherwise the newcomer brings a pilot of its own, drafted or one it made, and the route waits until it joins (`T.squadReady`). A late pilot catches up with the least: it gets as many spare upgrade points as the least-grown pilot already flying has grown past it (`T.joinPilot`, `T.pilotPower`), and its player's purse starts as full as the emptiest. It keeps the level it came with, and the next mission scales for the bigger squad (`docs/reference/SCALING.md`). Nobody joins during a mission, a reward, a haven or an event.

**P vs E (the v1.0 grammar, unchanged).** P deepens the Physical face's numbers — reliable, AP-fed. E awakens the Ethereal face — it cannot be played until its card has E 1 — and then scales its _capability_, something P can never buy at any level. A new hero sets out with at least two cards awakened (`T.RULES.START_AWAKE`): the pre-made heroes are written that way, and a drafted hero's first two E points go to its strongest signatures from the starting six UP (the player can move them; Confirm tops the count back up to two if UP is left). Hover a face to see its exact numbers at the hero's current P/E.

## The round (what the player actually does)

1. **Round start.** Spawn-nodes and Spawners emit. Every enemy chooses an intent — shown as an arrow and a label ("Cleave → Rosh (4)").
2. **Hero phase.** Each standing hero: track slides one column (cards come home), engines fire, draw 4 dice from the pool (3 plain and 1 element die its player chose) and roll them into the bank (see *Dice* below). Then act freely with either hero in any order: play card faces (pay AP, and etherions for an Ethereal face, many of which cost no AP since 2.84; the card goes to the track for its cooldown), walk (1 AP per edge; height nodes need Space verbs or the ramp), Interact (2 AP), Rally a downed ally (2 AP, back at 8), take a basic action that needs no card — **Strike** an adjacent foe for 2 (2 AP), **Brace** for Shield 1 until your next turn (1 AP), **Shove** an adjacent foe 1 node (2 AP), as often as AP allows and never onto the track; and, with two pilots, a duet move — **Spot** a foe up to 3 nodes away, so the next hit on it by anyone deals +2 (2 AP), **Leg up** an adjacent ally up to 2 nodes, to a node you choose, at once (1 AP), or **Lend** an adjacent ally 2 AP for this round (2 AP; lent after their turn, it is lost); **Channel** a banked etherion (or Omni) on its own, with no AP: Mass — Strike an adjacent foe for 2 or Shove it 1 node; Charge — Arc 1 damage into a foe up to 3 nodes away, or (1 AP) Reach an objective 2 nodes away; Space — Scramble 1 node, ledges included; Time — Brace and slide your track 1 extra. So a rolled crystal is never a dead die, even with no AP and no awakened card of its colour — use gear (◇ per use, own small cooldown), fold M+C+S+T into one Omni, or return a clogged die to the pool. Minted (temporary) dice evaporate at the end of the phase. Banked dice carry — but the pool shrinks, so hoarding starves next turn's roll (the v1.0 hard pool cap, intact).
3. **Enemy phase.** Each enemy executes its declared intent. If the target left range it closes distance instead; a zone shot against a Hidden hero strikes an afterimage; a pushed enemy's plan collapses. Kiters retreat before shooting. Lurkers ambush from Hidden. Harriers blink adjacent and strike.
4. **Round end.** Counted snares spring; decoys strike; hazards deal 3 true damage to anyone standing in them (enemies too — push them in); spores choke; dots tick; the beacon counts if a hero is near it; the clock ticks; the flood spreads; states expire; the Heart may crack.

### Dice (2.29)

Every pilot has 15 dice, and pool + banked = 15 always. Most are **plain d20s**: Mass, Charge, Space, Time and Omni once each, AP on the other 15 sides (0.75 AP and 0.25 etherion a die). Each card the pilot has awakened (E 1 or more), swaps one plain die for a **d8 of that element**: four sides its own element, one each of the other three and Omni, no AP (1 etherion a die, half of them its own colour). So a pilot who wakes more cards rolls fewer AP and more etherions, mostly of the colours its kit uses: the mage path. Each turn **four** dice are drawn from the pool and rolled: **three plain d20s, the AP dice, and one element die of the player's choice** (2.83; Garrett, 2026-11-21: "instead of picking your dice it should be 3 ap dice and 1 element of your choice"; it replaces 2.82's free choice of all four). The element die is one of the pilot's own: a d8 of an element it has awakened, or Omni Core's d12 (`T.RULES.ROLL_EL`, `T.setRollPick`, kept in `h.rollPick`). Choose under *Next roll*, beside the pilot's dice, at any time in a mission, on anyone's turn; the choice stays from roll to roll until changed. With nothing chosen, or every die of that kind banked, the roll takes the element die the pool holds most of; with no element die in the pool all four are plain, and with too few plain dice the rest are the element dice the pool holds most of. **No die is ever drawn at random** (2.101; Garrett, 2026-11-21): a hand in the bag feels a d20 from a d8, so every die that leaves the pool is one a player could pick by touch. Choosing a die never chooses its face. When players take turns the roll comes as a player's turn ends, so a choice made after it is for the roll after; all at once, everyone rolls as the round begins. The simulated players choose too (`T.autoRollPick`): the element of an Ethereal face back in hand next round that the bank cannot pay for, else the element their awakened cards use most. A banked die keeps its kind, and a spent one goes back to the pool. The symbols are Cosmic Shore's element petals (a key sits under the selected pilot's dice). The design and its open questions: `docs/design/10-dice.md`.

**Downed, not dead.** At 0 a hero is downed and loses states; an adjacent ally can Rally them. Both downed = mission lost.

## The route

Seven rows, drawn top to bottom since 2.70: the squad sets out at the top and the Heart waits at the foot. Row 0 offers three openers (Swarm / Vault / Cascade). Rows 1–5 mix missions, Elites, Havens (row 2 and a guaranteed Haven in row 4) and Events. Row 6 is the Heart. Each node links to 1–3 nodes in the next row; every node is reachable. Tiers: rows 0–1 tier 1 (_The Lowland Reach_), rows 2–4 tier 2 (_The Open Sea_), rows 5–6 tier 3 (_The High Air_). A refused privateer toll upgrades your next mission to an Elite; a paid guide skips a row.

**Length** (2.84, `T.LENGTHS`, `run.length`, chosen by the host before the route opens). _Full_ is the seven-row route above. _Short_ has five stops and keeps the same shape: three openers (tier 1), a tier-2 row of Hidden / Event / Salvage, a tier-2 row of Haven / Elite / Vault, a third tier-2 row of Survey / Haven / Swarm, then the Heart (tier 3). With fewer missions to grow on, Short is harder at the Heart (2.84, 80 runs each: the look-ahead player wins 35 Short against 50 Full, the heuristic 10 against 12; a tier-3 row before the Heart made it 6 for the heuristic). Each row's leg (and its ground and name) follows its tier (`T.actOf(row, map)`).

**The route screen reads like a climb** (2.86; Frogma "Finalised UI", Route 3): the Heart at the top with how many stops away it is, the legs still to come as bands of their art, the leg the squad is on with the stops it can reach next as big art cards, then every stop passed, newest first (a mission's line says whether it was won, in how many rounds and how many foes fell), down to the Ark. The squad's pilots stand in a column beside it, with Train and Kit. The seven-row chart below (the whole route, every road) stays, folded until opened. When a newcomer joins on the route, a card says who is bringing a pilot and the route waits for them.

The stops the squad can reach next stand side by side as cards (`T.previewStop`): for a mission, its objective in a word (Hold, Break in, Find, Channel, Carry, Tag, Defeat) and in full, its clock, its foes and any swarm, the place's gravity and weather, and its bonus goals. The preview reads the same spec the mission is launched from (`T.missionSpec`), so the numbers are the ones that will be played; it changes nothing in the run, and never shows what must be found in play (which cache is real). A stop the run owes as an elite says so.

## Difficulty: the wind

Chosen on the title screen before a run (`run.tide`, `T.TIDES` in `world.js`); the device remembers the last choice.

- **Steady wind** (`rising`) — the game as tuned through 2.6, and since 2.29 (four dice a turn) every objective needs 1 more and every clock but a hold's starts 1 round shorter, from tier 1. A Salvage takes at most one extra crate from any tide: a crate is a round trip.
- **Gale** (`flood`) — for careful players. As the steady wind, and from tier 2 every objective needs 1 more again (tier 3 too): one more round held, seal broken, cache dig or valve channel. Every clock except a hold's starts 1 round shorter again. Swarms grow 1 larger (2). Elites bring 1 more escort (2: a Kiter, then a Binder). Tier 1 and the Heart are never changed.

What each does to win rates is in `BALANCE.md` (v2.7.0).

## Rewards (after a won mission)

- **UP:** 2, +1 per bonus objective achieved, +1 for an Elite. Both heroes receive it; spend any time on the map via **Train**.
- **Omnicrystals (◇):** 4 + tier, +2 per bonus objective, +4 Elite, +10 boss (+2 Deep Pockets).
- **A trait for every hero** (since 2.24): each hero is offered 3 cards of their own, drawn from traits that hero could legally take (bloodline must match; 3-of-9 cap; 9 max), never a card offered to the other hero too. Each takes 1, or skips for +2 ◇. With two players, each chooses for their own pilot.
- Everyone binds wounds: **+25% vitality** after a won mission; downed heroes get up at 25%.

**Bonus objectives** are per-archetype (e.g. _no hero below 50%_, _destroy the Warden_, _finish with 3+ rounds to spare_, _take no ambush damage_, _take no hazard damage_, _both heroes standing_). They are the skill-expression reward loop.

## Lifeline

Once per run, losing a **non-boss** mission does not end the run: no rewards, both heroes limp on at 50%, the Lifeline is spent. Losing the boss, or losing again, ends the run.

## Omnicrystals and gear

◇ is the **managed economy** (v1.0: "etherions = weather, omnicrystals = supply"). It is both currency and **fuel**: gear abilities cost ◇ per use and have their own small cooldown (not the track). Each hero carries up to **2 gear**; Havens stock 3 at a time (prices 4–7 ◇), and you may sell for half.

**A purse per player** (since 2.39; Garrett, 2026-11-15). One player keeps one purse for both pilots, so a solo run plays exactly as before. Two players keep one each (`run.purses`, `src/engine/party.js`). The 6 ◇ a squad starts with are shared out when the route opens. A pilot buys gear, sells gear and fuels its gear in a mission from its own player's purse, and a skipped trait (+2 ◇) pays its own player. What the squad earns together (a mission's crystals, an event's) is shared out evenly, the odd crystal to the emptier purse. What it pays together (a haven's full repair, an event's price) comes from the fuller purse first. The audit reads the crystals together and halves every purse. Each screen shows only its own player's buy, sell and train buttons; the partner's purse and choices are shown, never offered.

Gear is ethereal technology (2.89, the keyword plan): it works on spaces, the dice, the clock and the downed, it is bought rather than trained, and it strikes only through a Field. Every piece has **one passive**, on while it is worn (what boons did until 2.89), and **two actives** paid in ◇ with their own small cooldown (consumables' jobs live here now).

| Gear | ◇ | Passive | Actives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winch | 6 | your Fields push and pull 1 further | Reel Line (a Field within 2 for 3 rounds: the first foe in is pulled 2 toward you, 1◇) · Winch (a Field within 3 and its neighbours for 2 rounds: the first 2 foes in are pulled 2 and Anchored 1, 2◇) |
| Scope | 5 | reveals reach 1 further; Hidden foes beside you are revealed as your turn begins | Glance (reveal 2, 1◇) · Survey (reveal all + find the hidden objective, 2◇) |
| Capacitor | 6 | start each mission with 1 AP banked | Store (draw a pool die as Omni, 1◇) · Discharge (mint 2 AP + 1 Charge, 2◇) |
| Phase-brace | 7 | your blinks reach 1 further | Blink (teleport 2, height ok, 2◇) · Fade (Hidden 1, 1◇) |
| Chrono-shim | 7 | the track slides 1 extra as round 2 begins | Shim (track +1, 1◇) · Rewind (track +3 and clock +2, 3◇) |
| Aegis Plate | 6 | every Shield you gain is +1 | Brace (Shield 3 all, 1 round, 1◇) · Bulwark (Shield 3 all, 2 rounds, you + adjacent ally, 2◇) |
| Storm Lantern | 5 | as your turn begins, a foe beside you is Marked | Flare (reveal 2 + Mark all revealed, 1◇) · Beacon (ally free move 2, 2◇) |
| Turret | 6 | your Fields last 1 round longer | Sentry (a Field within 2 and its neighbours for 2 rounds: 2 to every foe ending the round there, 1◇) · Skewer (a Field within 2 for 3 rounds: the first foe in takes 5, Pierce, 2◇) |
| Climbing Rig | 4 | mag boots: you cannot be pulled | Ascend (climb 1, height ok, 1◇) · Rappel (move 2 through foes, 1◇) |

Gear is the **orthogonality insurance** from the v1.0 mechanic basis: any build can buy the verb it lacks (Space for a vault, Reveal for the Sought, a Turret for a crowd) — general verbs, never keyed counters.

**Boons and consumables were folded into gear in 2.89.** Thick Hide is the Aegis Plate's passive, Keen Sense the Scope's, Quick Hands the Chrono-shim's, Second Wind the Medkit's, Anchor Soul the Climbing Rig's, and Etherion-touched (gentler: an AP) the Capacitor's. The Affinities and Omni Core are cut, since the element die is chosen every turn; Momentum and Defiant are cut. Rally Vial is the Medkit's Patch (a mending Field since 2.90), Grit the Aegis Plate's Brace, Flashpowder the Scope's Glance, Omni Shard the Capacitor's Store, the Windbreak Charm the Chrono-shim's Rewind. An older save refunds its boons (3 ◇ each) and consumables (their price).

**Havens:** rest for half of missing vitality free, or full repair for 3 ◇.

## Events (12)

Terminal Beacon · Privateer Toll · Etherion Storm · Bloom of the Superorganism · Stranded Guide · Drowned Cache · Lintukoto Shrine · Mirror Pool (respec) · The Heart's Envoy · Molt Season (swap a trait) · Terminal Audit (≥10 ◇ halved, else +4) · Quiet Hour. Each is 2–3 choices with costs, risks and occasional follow-ups (a card pick, a trade, crystals). No event repeats within a run.

## Saving

The run saves to `localStorage` at every node and after every action in a mission; **Continue** returns to wherever the page was closed, in the middle of a round if need be (`docs/reference/STATE_AND_SAVES.md`). Lifetime stats (tides sailed, Consolidations toppled, deepest row) persist across runs.
