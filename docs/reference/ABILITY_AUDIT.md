# Ability audit

> Generated by `npm run audit` (`tools/audit.js`). Do not edit by hand — change the game, or the tables at the top of the tool.
> 198 faces on 99 cards · 304 usage missions · 16 missions per gate cell · seeded and reproducible.

The rules this checks:

1. Every ability is useful somewhere in the journey.
2. No mission requires a particular ability — nothing is a gate.
3. No ability is a one-trick hard counter to one specific problem.
4. Every ability is either a **staple** (plain, useful, played often) or a flexible **tool** (answers several kinds of problem).

**How to read it.** Pass A is the audit’s *opinion* of each face, derived from its verb recipe: which of ten recurring problems it answers. Pass B is what the look-ahead player actually played. Pass C removes a whole family of answers from the card pool and replays every mission type. The look-ahead player knows no card by name: it tries every legal action on a copy of the mission, lets the declared enemy turn play out there, and keeps what scores best — so it can play any face, but it plans one round at a time and holds nothing back for later. Treat pass B as “where to look”: a face it never plays is weak for its cost, or pays off later than one round. Pass C is a fair test: if a careful player still wins without a family of answers, that family is not a gate.

**Limits.** Only trait cards are audited — gear and enemy abilities are not, yet. Pass A reads recipes, which is to say intent: where the engine does not honour a recipe option (see the known issues in `docs/HANDOFF.md`), the audit overstates.
