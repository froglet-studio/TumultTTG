# Tumult v2.0 — the game as it stands

> This describes what is implemented today. Where the game is headed is in `docs/ROADMAP.md` and `docs/design/`.
>
> **v2.0 turns the v1.0 proof-of-concept into a playable, replayable game**: a roguelike *tide-run* where you draft two heroes from eleven bloodlines, ride a branching Tide Map through a Consolidation's territory, and topple its Heart. The v1.0 core is kept whole — 15-die economy, 6-column track, the P/E scaling grammar, no turn theft, no hard counters — and the fun layers are built on top.

## Play it

`npm install`, then `npm run dev` (http://localhost:5173) or `npm run build` (→ `dist/TUMULT.html`, one self-contained file that works offline). Runs save automatically, in the browser's local storage, at every node and after every action in a mission: close the page mid-round and **Continue** picks up the same round.

**The playtest build** — `npm run build:playtest` (→ `dist/TUMULT.playtest.html`) or `http://localhost:5173/?playtest` — is the same game with a panel on the title screen for whoever is testing it:

- **Jump into a mission.** Any of the six archetypes at any tier (the Heart is always tier 3), two of the pre-made heroes, their starting six cards or the kit a squad would carry by that tier, and a seed. The seed, the mission and the tier fix the board and the dice to come, whichever squad and kit play it, and `node tools/mission-log.js <archetype> <tier> <seed> <heroA> <heroB> <start|tier>` prints that same mission, because both start from `T.quickMission`. When the mission ends, the Tide carries on from that node.
- **Way to play with friends** (2.81, in place of the Ethereal rule switch kept from 2.4 to 2.80). **Take turns** (as shipped) or **All at once** (`run.party.rounds`, see Players below). Expeditions started on that screen play that way, and so does the one under way when that screen is its host; the choice stays set on that device.
- **What to look at in this build.** A short list, rewritten every release; a test fails if its version is not the package's.
- **Report.** On the title screen and in a mission's top bar: the mission in progress (seed, squad, foes, the last lines of the log, the command that rebuilds its board) and every mission finished on that device, with how many Physical and Ethereal faces were played and how many of those at E 0. It is kept in the browser's local storage (`tumult_v2_playtest`) and goes nowhere unless someone copies it. **Copy save** copies the Tide exactly as it stands, a mission in progress included; `node tools/mission-log.js --save <file>` carries on from that moment.
- **Plan.** In a mission's top bar: what the look-ahead player would do with the turn, step by step, and a button to play it. It is the player the balance tables are measured with ([`PLAYERS.md`](PLAYERS.md)), so this is where to check whether its judgement is any good.

The ordinary build carries none of this. No screen scrolls sideways at a phone's width, but the mission screen is still built for a mouse: a card face's numbers are in its hover tooltip (roadmap P1).

## The pitch, one paragraph

Every round both heroes act freely and interleaved. Each hero's track slides, engines fire, and they roll 4 dice into their bank. **Enemies have already told you what they will do** — intents are drawn on the board as arrows — so each round is a solvable puzzle: step out of a zone and the shot has no line; move two nodes and the slow brute spends its turn walking; Hide and ranged attacks strike afterimages; Anchor a Brute and it cannot close the distance. Cards have two faces: a cheap Physical face that scales with **P**, and an Ethereal face that costs an etherion and does what physical cannot — teleport, chain, area, control, engines — scaling with **E**. An Ethereal face is magic that must be awakened: it cannot be played until its card has E 1, and every hero sets out with at least two cards awakened. Between missions you earn Upgrade Points and omnicrystals, claim new traits (3-of-9 per bloodline), buy gear that patches your verb gaps, and pick up boons. At the top of the map, the Heart.

## What's new vs v1.0 (the fun layers)

1. **You play it.** The demos were inspectors; this is a game with agency, stakes, and a 30–60 minute run arc.
2. **The draft.** Hero creation is a real pack draft — pick 1 of 4, six times, across your three bloodline pools — or Quick Build one of six pre-made heroes.
3. **The Tide Map.** Seven rows, three acts (*The Shallows, The Undertow, The Deep*), branching paths with missions, elites, havens, events and the boss.
4. **Generated missions from the four challenge archetypes** — Swarm (hold the beacon), Vault (height + the winch lever), The Sought (hidden caches, lurkers), Cascade (spreading flood, the valve) — plus Elite and a two-phase Boss. *v1.0 roadmap item #1 (scenario generator): done in first form.*
5. **Telegraphed enemy intents** with minimal adaptation (they close distance if you leave range). Into-the-Breach legibility on top of the v1.0 "no reactive defense" rule.
6. **Progression & economy.** UP earning, 3-of-9 trait growth with card rewards, omnicrystals as both currency and gear fuel, 10 gear items, each with a passive and two actives (since 2.89 they carry what boons and consumables did), havens, a Lifeline.
7. **All 99 cards rewritten in the v1.0 two-face formula format and implemented** as data-driven verb recipes. *v1.0 roadmap item #2: done.*
8. **A bestiary of 12 enemy types** built from the same verbs, with per-role AI policies.
9. **Balance pass** in the v1.0 spirit: lethality budget, approach taxed (teleports are Ethereal faces: an etherion and a cooldown of 2–4), missions tuned toward the 8–14 round band, hundreds of simulated missions and full runs.
10. **Hero vitality 26** (the engine's value), downed-not-dead with Rally, one Lifeline per run.

## Design pillars (unchanged from v1.0, now load-bearing)

- **Small core, enormous space.** A fixed vocabulary of verbs and states, four etherion surfaces, two faces per card. Depth comes from combination; a special-case rule is a smell.
- **No hard counters.** Every state is pressured by several general verbs. Gear grants verbs, never "anti-X".
- **No turn theft.** Control taxes efficiency or position (Anchored still acts; Weakened hits softer; Slow can't move). The player always acts.
- **Predictable outcomes; surprise from timing and combination.** Deterministic combat, random replenishment, visible intents.
- **Balance by contribution, not symmetry.** A kit should be the best answer to some archetype and a weak one to another. Strikers crack Wardens and the Heart; controllers own swarms and clocks.

## Lore frame (light touch — for the lore team to refine)

Each run is an **expedition on Tumult** (since 2.16): a squad of guides set down from an **Ark**, crossing land, sea and air along one route to **the Heart** at its end. Each expedition is drawn from the run's seed (`src/data/expeditions.js`, since 2.17): who sent it (a Jade appointment, a Ruby venture or a Gold contract), what the Heart is this time (its rules never change), and the order and ground of the three legs. Between stops the run writes short passages in the pilots' voices (`src/data/story.js`). The **Terminal** is the quest-giver that will not intervene ("helps those who help themselves" — and logs the transaction). Etherion storms grant Affinities; the superorganism's reef blooms offer traits; privateers levy tolls the Terminal tolerates as redistribution; an audit halves hoarded crystals. All event text lives in `src/data/world.js` under `T.EVENTS`. The frame is due to change — see `docs/design/01-frame-and-campaign.md`.

## Players

One to five players (title screen). Each hero belongs to a player, its seat (`run.party`, `src/engine/party.js`): with one player, both; with more, one each. Seat 0, the host, makes the squad's shared choices: the route on the map, skipping a reward card, resting and leaving a haven, an event's option, finishing a mission. Each player drafts, trains, equips and flies only their own pilot. A round is played one of two ways (`run.party.rounds`; the host chooses, on the playtest panel). **Take turns** (`'turns'`, 2.81; Garrett, 2026-11-20): in a mission's hero phase one player at a time flies their pilot and ends their turn, and the enemy turn comes once every player has had theirs. The order is the players' to choose each round: whoever acts first for one of their pilots begins their turn (`party.active`), and nobody else acts until it ends (so a duo power stays the acting player's to plan); a player may also end their turn without acting. Ending a turn rolls that player's next dice at once (`T.rollAhead`, marked `rolledAhead` until the round begins), so they plan while the others play. A player whose pilots are all down has no turn (`T.seatsToPlay`, `T.turnSeat`, `T.turnOpen`). **All at once** (`'live'`): no turns. Every player spends their AP whenever they like, in whatever order they agree at the table, and presses Done (pressed again, Waiting takes it back); the enemy turn comes once every player with a pilot standing is done, and everyone rolls together as the next round begins, as before 2.81 (`T.seatsPlaying`). Online, the host's screen carries out commands one at a time, so two moves made at the same moment are taken in the order they arrive, and one the board no longer allows is refused. In either way a line of story may be written at any time. A command for a pilot whose player may not act now is refused. The rules never change with the number of players. `src/ui/party.js` routes clicks: `U.seats` is the list of seats a screen holds, `null` for all of them.

Every change a player makes goes through a command, `T.execCommand(run, { op, hero, … }, seats)`, which refuses one for another player's pilot. **Online** (test page only, `src/ui/net.js`): the host's screen holds the game and writes the whole run to the page's shared store (`games/<CODE>`) after every change; a guest's screen shows it, drafts its own pilot locally, and sends its commands (`games/<CODE>/cmds`), which the host carries out in order. Commands are numbered per seat (2.73.1): the host carries out number n only after n − 1 and writes the last one it carried out for each seat (`acks`) with the state. A guest also carries out each command on its own copy as it sends it, so its screen answers at once; the host's state still wins, and every command the host has not acknowledged is played again on top of each state it writes, however long the host takes, and written again every few seconds until it is. The host reads the commands live and also every 2.5 s, since the store's live delivery can lag or stop; a guest whose commands wait reads the state itself, and after 8 s says it is waiting for the host's screen. Where the page offers a live channel (claude.ai's `room`, 2.74), the game's screens also meet in a room named `tumult-<CODE>`: a guest sends each command there too, and the host announces each state it writes (`rev`), so the other screen hears within a moment; the store stays the truth, and a guest reads the state at least every 2 s. A screen remembers its game on the device (`tumult_v2_net`), so after a reload the title offers to rejoin it, as guest or as host. The title lists the games being played (2.80): each host's screen keeps a small entry in `lobby/<CODE>` (host, players, pilots, where the expedition is, whether a newcomer can take a seat now) and refreshes it at least once a minute, so a newcomer sees who is playing and joins with one click, no code needed; a game not heard from for three minutes is folded away as quiet, one finished is shown as finished, and the entry is deleted when the host leaves. Codes still work. A partner needs write access to the page (an email invite as Editor, or Contributor in the organization); a view-only link can watch the title but cannot host or join, and the title says so. Because a run is plain data and the engine deterministic, the state is the whole truth: there is nothing to reconcile.

## Keyboard / UI notes

Tab switches the selected hero; E ends the round; Esc cancels targeting; Ctrl+Z (⌘Z) takes back the last move. Hover any card face, die, state chip or token for exact numbers at the hero's current P/E. The hint bar above the board says how much AP the selected hero has. Since 2.71 the pilot being flown sits in the dock along the foot of the screen, which never folds or scrolls away: its vitality, its AP in large type, its etherions banked, its dice, the track, its whole hand in one row, the basic actions and gear, and (2.75) a button for each other pilot, to fly them. The side column holds what changes: the enemy intents, the ally (folded to its vitality, AP and dice; click to fly it, which swaps it into the dock, or Show cards to look at its hand) and the log. The log, the enemy intents and a long objective fold, and the screen remembers which (`tumult_v2_ui`). When the objective moves forward a gold card on the board says what was done and how far along it is; Interact glows when it would count, and a play that would move the objective carries a gold ◎.

**The HUD** (2.86; Frogma "Finalised UI": H-Final, G-HUD5, G-Live, G-Pass). The board sits in a frame with the sea around it (HUD 1×2). Across the top, on the water: the mission's kind, tier, place and conditions, its name, the objective with a progress bar and the bonuses; then the clock with the round under it, the purse, Undo, ✎, ☰ and End. Under End, one line says who still has a turn and whose turn ends the round. Inside the frame, along its foot: the hint for the pilot being flown and the forecast. The right column holds the foes' plans (each with its vitality bar; a long list scrolls inside its panel), the **squad** and the log. The squad lists every pilot, its player, vitality, etherions and AP, and where its player stands this round: whose turn it is, who is done, who still has one to take (any order), or, in live rounds, who is still playing and how many are done; click a row to fly that pilot, or Cards to look at its hand. The dock along the foot: the pilot (portrait, vitality, AP, etherions, pool, dice), the track as a strip over the hand (Ready, then next round, in 2 … in 6), and the actions. **⛶ Full screen** (HUD 1+) lets the board fill the screen; the plans and the hand become trays that slide away (Plans ▸, Hand ▾), and ⤡ goes back to the frame. Online, a player who has ended their turn (or is done, in live rounds) sees who the table is waiting on, and in live rounds can take it back. When a player drops, their squad row says so with the countdown before an AI pilot takes the seat, and a card at the top of the side column repeats it with the host's choices (give the seat to the AI now, or wait); a seat the AI holds or fills says so in its row. **Passing the screen**: when players take turns at one screen, a cover goes over the board between turns. It names who is up and hides the last hand until its player has the screen again; when several players still have a turn, it asks who goes next. The shape chosen (frame or full screen, the trays, the phone's tab) is kept with the folds in `tumult_v2_ui`.

**The look (2.76).** Behind every screen is a moving sea (`src/ui/sea.js`): sea lines, slow light and rising motes. By default it follows the expedition down the route, from Shore in the shallows through Deep Sea and Glow to Storm at the Heart, darker every stop; **Look** pins one of the four themes, picks the accent of the call to act, and sets motion to Full, Reduced or Off (it follows the device's reduce-motion setting until a player picks; kept in `tumult_v2_look`). On a mission, the screen's own buttons sit behind ☰, gravity, weather and the hour fold into one *conditions* chip, the story box opens from ✎, every action that can be taken now shows, with the ones that cannot under *Can't do now* and an action paid two ways (Strike for AP or a Mass) as one button that asks how to pay, a card with nothing to do folds small, and the how-to line shows in the first two missions only. With motion on, tokens walk their path node by node, a striker leans into its blow and the target shakes, the forecast chip that changed pulses, the track steps toward Ready at a new round, and screens slide in. None of it reads or writes a rule.

**The forecast.** In a strip under the board (2.75; before, under the enemy intents), a chip a pilot answers *what happens if I end the round now?* — what each hero takes or mends, how many foes fall, whether the objective advances, whether the mission ends — and each hero's vitality shows where it is heading (`26/26 → 20`). It adds the telegraphs up properly: a foe that cannot reach does not count, shields and hazards do. An enemy turn has no dice in it, so the forecast is exact (`T.forecast`, tested against what then happens). While a play is previewed, the strip shows what ending the round would do *after* that play, with what changes ringed and the old figure struck through.

**Previews.** Hovering a face, a basic action or a gear ability plays it on a copy of the mission (`T.imagine`) and draws the result on the board: damage, states, who moves where, who falls, and the forecast after it. On a touch screen, which cannot hover, the first tap on a play is the preview (the button is ringed) and the second tap makes it.

**Undo** (2.75). Any move can be taken back, with the ↶ Undo button beside End Round or Ctrl+Z, until something comes out that the player could not have known: a die rolled, anything drawn at random, a cache searched, a veiled mark or a Hidden foe seen, a foe arriving, the mission or the round ending (`T.canTakeBack`, `T.secretsOf`). A move that does any of those clears what could be taken back before it, so undoing never re-rolls or un-sees anything. Lines of story written since stay. Online games have no Undo, since the host's mission is shared.

**Screen sizes** (2.75). On a tablet or a phone the screen is one column that never runs off the side: the top bar, the board sized to its map, the forecast strip, the dock, then the intents, the ally and the log. The hand is swiped, a card at a time, and every play is at least 40 px tall on a touch screen. On a phone the top bar puts End Round and Undo beside the mission's name and folds the objective to one line; the story box is left to larger screens. Since 2.86 the foot of a phone or small tablet is one sheet: the pilot being flown with its AP, then tabs (Hand, Plans, Squad, Log) with End beside them, then the tab's pane; the grip at the top of the sheet folds it to the pilot and the tabs.
